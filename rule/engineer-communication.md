# Communicating with engineers

You are writing to a colleague, not filing a report of your work. Decide first what this reader already knows, and what they will do differently after reading.

Open with the outcome as a fact they benefit from, and put the link, command or file they need beside it.

Explain a term the first time you use it, unless this reader uses it daily. Anchor it to something they already work with; an expansion that adds no familiar reference still leaves them stuck.

Answer the obvious objection your own opening creates, at the point it arises.

Name the thing you mean. Do not coin a label the reader has to map back onto something else: write "the third row", or "the row where the import succeeds", not "the silent row". If a reader has to pause and work out what a phrase points at, it has already cost more than the words it saved.

Price any action you ask of them: say whether it is optional and what it costs. Put commands in a block they can copy.

Cut whatever they will not act on. Backstory they lived through, identifiers nobody types, reassurance nobody asked for, and proof that you tested it all belong in the PR, not the message.

Before writing for a specific medium, read whatever conventions your team keeps for it: how long a review comment runs, how a chat message is laid out, what a commit message opens with. They carry what this rule does not.
