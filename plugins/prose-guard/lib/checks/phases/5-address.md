Is this written to the people who will actually read it?

It FAILS only if you can point to one of these, quoting the span:

(a) "you" or "your" pointing at somebody specific to this exchange — the reviewer, the person who
    asked, whoever raised the objection — where the destination is a group or an open set: a pull
    request body saying "the two files worth your review", a documentation page saying "you asked for
    this", an announcement saying "your comment was right". Most readers are not that person and
    cannot tell whether it means them. Second person addressed to every reader alike is not this:
    "you give it an audience", "you will install something new next month", "you have all watched a
    deploy go out on a Friday" name nobody in particular, so nobody is left wondering whether it means
    them;
(b) an individual addressed as though present — "you both praised", "David, your objection" — in
    something that will be read later by people who were not there;
(c) an assumption that the reader took part in the conversation that produced this: "as we
    discussed", "as you know", "per your comment", "the thing you raised";
(d) a fact the destination already displays beside the message: which branch a pull request targets,
    how many files it changed, which message a threaded reply sits under.

**Second person is correct wherever the message has one obvious addressee, and most messages do.** A
direct message, a reply in a thread, a comment on someone's change, an answer to a question — there
"you" resolves to one person and every reader knows which. Never fail those.

Naming a person is also fine where the point is who did something and the reader can act on knowing
it. What fails is addressing them as though they were the audience.

Out of scope for you: anything present in order to satisfy a different check. In particular, an
explanation of a technical term is never padding, and the job of the opening sentence is fixed.
Judge only your own concern. If your concern is already satisfied, or satisfying it would mean
undoing something the message is doing deliberately, answer PASS.

not more ceremony. Anything readable outside the company must avoid internal shorthand and
